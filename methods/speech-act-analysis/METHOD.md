# Speech-Act and Language-in-Use Analysis

Status: **preview and unevaluated**

Use this method when a statement matters because of what it does in context—not only because of
what its words describe. It can distinguish an assertion from a request, promise, warning,
permission, refusal, declaration, or indirect act and trace the commitments that follow.

## What it checks

1. The exact utterance, participants, sequence, and institutional context.
2. What the wording says versus what action it may perform.
3. Whether authority, conventions, timing, and other enabling conditions are present.
4. Observed uptake, commitments, effects, and credible alternative readings.

## Important limit

The method cannot read private intention or manufacture evidence of audience response. Classifying
an utterance does not make it true, effective, legitimate, or legally binding.

The canonical contract is [method.yaml](method.yaml). It is a bounded project adaptation informed
by the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on [Speech Acts](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2023/entries/speech-acts/index.html).
