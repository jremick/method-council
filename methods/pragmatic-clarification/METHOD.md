# Pragmatic Clarification

Status: **preview and unevaluated**

Use this method when a concept, hypothesis, or disagreement has unclear practical meaning. It asks
what experience, observation, rational action, or decision would actually differ if each candidate
meaning were accepted.

## Procedure shape

1. Bound the competing claims or meanings and affected decision.
2. Define the supported contexts and goals.
3. Trace observable, experiential, inquiry, and action consequences.
4. Identify observations that could discriminate between candidates.
5. Separate shared consequences from material differences.
6. Diagnose what remains empirical, normative, interpretive, or merely verbal.

## Adaptation and limits

This is a bounded project adaptation of Peirce's Pragmatic Maxim. It clarifies practical meaning; it
does not establish that a useful belief is true or reduce all moral, experiential, or metaphysical
meaning to immediate utility.
